# Puiseux–Laurent recursion

> Authority: `MUTABLE_NONAUTHORITATIVE`  
> Labels: `NTLC-01` and `NTLC-03`

## 1. Normal-form theorem

Let `F/C` be a one-variable function field with constant field `C`, let
`R=F((t))`, and let `x,y in R` have finite negative Laurent parts. Assume

```text
v_t(x)=-m<0,
Omega=dx wedge dy in t^e (dt/t) wedge Omega^1_(F/C)[[t]],
e>0.                                                (1.1)
```

### Theorem `NTLC-01`

After a finite algebraic extension `F'/F`, there is a uniformizer
`s=t times a unit` such that

```text
x=s^(-m).                                           (1.2)
```

Writing

```text
y=sum_j c_j s^j,  c_j in F',                       (1.3)
```

one has

```text
d c_j=0, hence c_j in C, for every j<m+e.          (1.4)
```

If the zero-residue equation for `P dQ+y dx=dH` holds with `P,Q` regular, then

```text
c_m=0.                                              (1.5)
```

### Proof

Write `x=a t^(-m)(1+O(t))`. Adjoin an `m`-th root of `a`; the binomial series
for `x^(-1/m)` defines a uniformizer `s` and gives (1.2). Since `s=t u` for a
unit `u in F'[[t]]`, `s`- and `t`-orders agree. More precisely,

```text
dt/t=unit times ds/s+tangential terms.
```

Wedge products of two tangential one-forms vanish because `trdeg_C F'=1`.
Therefore a two-form of `t`-order at least `e` still has `s`-order at least
`e`; the parameter change cannot create a lower radial term.

Now

```text
dx=-m s^(-m) ds/s,
dy=sum_j s^j(j c_j ds/s+d c_j),
```

and therefore

```text
dx wedge dy=-m sum_j s^(j-m) ds/s wedge d c_j.     (1.6)
```

Different exponents cannot cancel. Condition (1.1) forces `d c_j=0` whenever
`j-m<e`. A finite extension of a function field over algebraically closed `C`
has no new constants, so those coefficients lie in `C`.

The logarithmic radial residue of `y dx` is `-m c_m`. A regular one-form
`P dQ` has zero radial residue, and an exact differential `dH` has zero radial
residue. Equation `P dQ+y dx=dH` therefore gives `c_m=0`. `square`

## 2. Full triangular Jacobian recursion

Expand the target form in the normalized coordinate:

```text
Omega=sum_(r>=e) s^r ds/s wedge beta_r,
beta_r in Omega^1_(F'/C).                          (2.1)
```

Comparing (1.6) with (2.1) gives the complete recursion

```text
d c_(m+r)=-(1/m) beta_r             for all r,     (2.2)
```

where `beta_r=0` for `r<e`. Thus:

- coefficients below `m+e` are constants;
- at each order `r>=e`, existence is equivalent to exactness of `beta_r` in
  the rational differential module of `F'`;
- when it exists, `c_(m+r)` is determined up to an additive constant in `C`;
- there are no hidden cross terms after the source-pole normalization.

This is an all-order formal criterion. It does not prove that the infinitely
many exactness conditions follow from a finite amount of global Keller data.
For a fixed finite truncation, it is an exact finite linear integration
problem.

## 3. Primitive recursion

Write in the same `s` coordinate

```text
P dQ=sum_r (C_r ds/s+D_r)s^r,
H=sum_r h_r s^r.
```

Because `x=s^(-m)`,

```text
y dx=-m sum_r c_(m+r) s^r ds/s.                   (3.1)
```

The equation `P dQ+y dx=dH` is therefore

```text
r h_r=C_r-m c_(m+r),                              (3.2)
d h_r=D_r.                                        (3.3)
```

For `r!=0`, (3.2) determines `h_r`, and (3.3) is compatible precisely because
of the Jacobian equation. At `r=0`, the two independent conditions are

```text
C_0-m c_m=0,
D_0=d h_0.                                        (3.4)
```

Regularity of `P,Q` gives `C_0=0`; hence (3.4) recovers `c_m=0`. The tangential
coefficient is

```text
D_0=Pbar d Qbar,                                  (3.5)
```

which must be exact. This is the branch Liouville obstruction proved globally
in `CONDUCTOR_GLUING.md`.

## 4. Leading equation and higher principal parts

If `y` has leading pole order `n`, then `c_(-n) in C*`. Translating
`s=t a^(-1/m)(1+O(t))` back to `t` gives

```text
b^m/a^n in C*,
```

which is equivalent to `n a' b-m a b'=0` and yields the gcd-reduced common
power described in `BOUNDARY_NORMALIZATION.md`.

The theorem also explains the earlier observation that exactness determines
but need not kill higher principal parts. Every nonresonant radial coefficient
of `H` is determined by (3.2); only the logarithmic resonance `r=0` must vanish.
The first nonconstant coefficient of `y` occurs at `s^(m+e)` and integrates the
leading ramification differential `beta_e`.

## 5. Limits

The normalization uses a finite coefficient extension and a formal uniformizer.
It does not imply that `s`, the coefficients `c_j`, or the resulting primitive
are algebraic functions on all of `Y`, polynomial in `x,y`, compatible at every
puncture, or generated by a target algebraic action. Those are separate
algebraization, conductor, and global-support conditions.
