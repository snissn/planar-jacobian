# Definitions and Conventions

## 1. Ground ring and Jacobian sign

Work in `C[x,y]`; the calculation is valid over any characteristic-zero field.
The Jacobian convention is

```text
J(A,B)=A_x B_y-A_y B_x.
```

A normalized Keller pair satisfies `J(P,Q)=1`.

## 2. Positive weights and actual defect

Let `w=(p,q)` be primitive with `p,q>0`, and set

```text
deg_w(x)=p,  deg_w(y)=q.
```

Write `d_P=deg_w(P)` and `d_Q=deg_w(Q)`. The actual defect is

```text
kappa_w(P,Q)=d_P+d_Q-p-q.
```

“Actual” means degrees are recomputed from the nonzero coefficients after every
transformation or cancellation. A partial cancellation on an exposed face does
not lower the defect.

## 3. Complete Rees layers

Write

```text
P=sum_(i>=0) P_i,  P_i homogeneous of weight d_P-i,
Q=sum_(j>=0) Q_j,  Q_j homogeneous of weight d_Q-j.
```

If the semigroup generated by `p,q` contains no monomial of the required
degree, that layer is the zero polynomial. It remains a layer and is not
removed from the index sequence.

For defect six, define

```text
S_s=sum_(i+j=s) J(P_i,Q_j).
```

The complete Rees equations are

```text
S_0=...=S_5=0,  S_6=1.
```

Terms with `i+j>6` have negative weighted degree and vanish individually.

## 4. Top common-power data

Since `S_0=J(P_0,Q_0)=0` and both top forms are nonconstant, the weighted
common-power lemma gives

```text
P_0=A H^m,  Q_0=B H^n,
A,B in C*,  gcd(m,n)=1,
```

with weighted-homogeneous nonconstant `H`. The maximal common-root convention
is used: `(m,n)` is coprime even when a nonmaximal root could be displayed.
This packet treats only `(m,n)=(2,3)` and `(3,2)`.

Put `rho=deg_w(H)`. Then `d_P=m rho` and `d_Q=n rho`.

## 5. Constant-bracket position

The scalar equation `S_6=1` implies at least one pair `(a,b)`, `a+b=6`, with

```text
J(P_a,Q_b)=c in C*.
```

The weighted degrees of `P_a,Q_b` are exactly `{p,q}`. The scalar `c` is kept
as a variable throughout. It may become `1` only when the complete `S_6`
equation forces that value; it is never normalized away without compensation.

## 6. Declared determinant-one transformations

Only the following orientation and graded-normalization maps are used.

### Source signed swap

```text
sigma(x,y)=(y,-x),  det Dsigma=1.
```

It exchanges the source weight order after relabeling `(p,q)` as `(q,p)`.

### Target signed swap

```text
tau(P,Q)=(Q,-P),  det Dtau=1,
J(Q,-P)=J(P,Q).
```

It exchanges the component order, the exponent pair, and the constant-bracket
position.

### Scalar-retaining graded source normalization

After the two signed swaps, take `p<=q` and arrange that `P_a` has degree `p`
and `Q_b` has degree `q`.

For `p<q`, the constant-bracket classification gives

```text
P_a=alpha x,
Q_b=beta y+h(x),
alpha beta=c,
```

where `h` is weighted homogeneous of degree `q`. Precompose first with

```text
(x,y)->(x,y-h(x)/beta)
```

and then with

```text
(x,y)->(x/alpha,alpha y).
```

Both maps have determinant one and preserve every weighted layer. The selected
layers become

```text
P_a=x,  Q_b=c y.
```

For `p=q`, primitivity gives `(p,q)=(1,1)`. If the selected linear pair has
matrix `M` and determinant `c`, precompose by

```text
M^(-1) diag(1,c),
```

whose determinant is one. The same normal form results. No target scalar
rescaling is used.

## 7. Transition terminology

A positive wall is a primitive positive normal exposing an edge of at least one
Newton polygon. Two fan chambers/faces are adjacent when they meet at such a
wall.

For the two components separately:

- a **shared transition** means both exposed edges contain the anchor vertex;
- a **P-only** or **Q-only** transition means only that component develops an
  edge at the first wall;
- an **axis transition** has an anchor vertex on a coordinate axis;
- an **origin transition** has the origin as the shared component vertex.

The four normal forms below have monomial common root, hence nonzero axis
anchors. The source signed swap supplies the opposite-axis orientation. No
origin anchor occurs.

## 8. Local shear versus global lexicographic descent

A determinant-one target shear cancelling an entire top component is a local
complete-top descent certificate. It contradicts the globally minimizing
record only after the transformed pair and a primitive weight are shown to have
strictly smaller complete lexicographic record.

No such distinction is needed in the final contradiction here: the four local
Rees systems do not exist. The packet nevertheless does not identify an
ordinary face shear with a globally lowering transformation.

## 9. Saturated coefficient ideal

For equations `I` and declared nonzero coefficients `u_1,...,u_r`, the checker
tests

```text
I : (u_1...u_r)^infinity
```

by adjoining `z u_1...u_r-1`. A Gröbner basis containing `1` proves that the
coefficient system has no solution with all declared coefficients nonzero.
Coefficients are collected only when their Jacobian terms have identical
exponent vectors.
