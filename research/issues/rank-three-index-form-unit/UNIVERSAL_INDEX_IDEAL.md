# Universal Index Ideal: What It Proves and What It Does Not

```text
claims: IDX3U-03, IDX3U-04
status: CANDIDATE_PROVED_WITH_NEGATIVE_CORRECTION
```

## 1. Intrinsic coefficient/content ideal

Let

```text
c_Phi: Sym^3(E) -> det(E)
```

be the polarized intrinsic binary cubic. Its image is a rank-at-most-one
submodule of the invertible module `det(E)`. Define the intrinsic content ideal
by

```text
J_Phi = im(c_Phi) tensor det(E)^(-1) subset B.                     (1.1)
```

Equivalently,

```text
J_Phi = Fitt^B_0(coker(c_Phi)).                                   (1.2)
```

After choosing a frame and writing

```text
Phi(X,Y)=aX^3+bX^2Y+cXY^2+dY^3,
```

one has

```text
J_Phi=(a,b,c,d).                                                   (1.3)
```

Equations (1.1)-(1.3) show that `J_Phi` is independent of the frame. Under a
frame change, the map `c_Phi` is conjugated by isomorphisms of its source and
target, so its image ideal and Fitting ideal are unchanged.

## 2. The ideal generated by all values

Define

```text
I_values = (Phi(s): s in E) tensor det(E)^(-1) subset B.           (2.1)
```

Then

```text
I_values = J_Phi.                                                  (2.2)
```

One inclusion is immediate from the coefficient expansion. Conversely, in a
frame let `f=aX^3+bX^2Y+cXY^2+dY^3`. The four evaluations

```text
f(1,0)=a,
f(0,1)=d,
f(1,1)=a+b+c+d,
f(1,-1)=a-b+c-d
```

recover

```text
b=(f(1,1)-f(1,-1)-2f(0,1))/2,
c=(f(1,1)+f(1,-1)-2f(1,0))/2.
```

Since `2` is invertible, every coefficient lies in the value-generated ideal.
This also gives a finite coefficient test.

## 3. Decisive correction: unit content is not unit representation

The primary objective asks for

```text
there exists s in E with Phi(s) in C*.                            (3.1)
```

This is **strictly stronger** than

```text
J_Phi=B.                                                          (3.2)
```

Condition (3.2) says that the coefficients generate the unit ideal, or
geometrically that the cubic section is not identically zero in any fiber. It
says that the generator open

```text
D(Phi) subset V(E)
```

is fiberwise nonempty. Condition (3.1) asks for a global section of that open.
A surjective open morphism need not have a section.

The issue #3 countermodel is already an exact counterexample to the implication
(3.2) => (3.1):

```text
Phi(X,Y)=-(uX^3+X^2Y+vY^3).
```

Its `X^2Y` coefficient is `-1`, so `J_Phi=B`, but the issue #3 specialization
argument proves that no `X,Y in C[u,v]` give a nonzero constant value.

Consequently, derivative simplicity applied only to `J_Phi` cannot solve the
rank-three unit-value problem even if full derivative stability were proved.
The global ledger wording must distinguish:

```text
universal coefficient ideal J_Phi = B            (insufficient),
fixed-section index Fitt_0(O/B[s]) = B            (equivalent to Phi(s) unit).
```

## 4. Derivations and all denominators

Let `D` be one of the target derivations `partial_P`, `partial_Q`, extended
uniquely from `K` to the finite separable field `L`. It is not assumed that
`D(O) subset O`.

Choose a finite `B`-basis of `O`. Since each `D(e_i)` belongs to
`L=O tensor_B K`, a common nonzero denominator exists:

```text
h_D in B\{0},
h_D D(O) subset O.                                                (4.1)
```

The operator `h_D D` is a derivation of `L`, restricts to `h_D partial_D` on
`B`, and preserves `O`. It also preserves `E`: trace commutes with derivations
in a finite separable extension, so trace-zero elements remain trace-zero.

Multiplication and the unit are horizontal for this denominator-cleared
connection. Therefore the cubic tensor is horizontal, and in any frame its
coefficient ideal satisfies

```text
h_D partial_D(J_Phi) subset J_Phi.                                (4.2)
```

For the two target directions, there are possibly different denominators:

```text
h_P partial_P(J_Phi) subset J_Phi,
h_Q partial_Q(J_Phi) subset J_Phi.                                (4.3)
```

This is the exact unconditional stability statement obtained here. It does not
permit cancellation of `h_P,h_Q` along their zero divisors.

If the stronger condition `D_P(O),D_Q(O) subset O` held, then one could take
`h_P=h_Q=1`, and derivative simplicity of `C[P,Q]` would imply every nonzero
differential ideal is `B`. That is the separate stable-order route. In a
ramified DVR, a transverse target derivation cannot preserve the full integral
closure, so this stronger hypothesis cannot be inserted silently.

## 5. Local monogenic denominator formula

On a monogenic chart `O_p=B_p[s]` with monic equation `f(T)`, differentiating
`f(s)=0` gives

```text
f'(s) D(s) = -D_B(f)(s).                                          (5.1)
```

The denominator is the local different generator `f'(s)`. Formula (5.1) is the
precise reason canonical derivations can acquire poles at ramification.
Multiplying by the different clears this one local chart. It does **not** prove
that a single global discriminant factor makes the unweighted ideal `J_Phi`
stable, nor does it produce a unit value.

## 6. Differential of the cubic tensor

In the generic determinant line,

```text
D Phi(s)
 = 1 wedge D(s) wedge s^2
   + 2(1 wedge s wedge s D(s)).                                   (6.1)
```

If `omega` is a chosen determinant frame with `D(omega)=kappa_D omega` and
`Phi(s)=phi(s) omega`, then

```text
D(phi(s)) + kappa_D phi(s)
```

is the scalar of the right side of (6.1). The connection coefficient
`kappa_D`, the coordinates of `D(s)`, and their clearing denominators are all
part of the formula; omitting them is equivalent to assuming stability of
`O`.

For a generically primitive `s`, the discriminant identity gives the logarithmic
relation, wherever defined,

```text
2 D(Phi(s))/Phi(s)
 = D(disc(B[s]))/disc(B[s]) - D(disc(O))/disc(O).                 (6.2)
```

This tracks excess contact but does not show that its divisor is empty.

## 7. Outcome of attack A

Successes:

- frame-independent intrinsic definition;
- exact Fitting interpretation;
- equality of coefficient and all-value ideals;
- denominator-cleared derivative stability (4.3).

Failure:

- `J_Phi=B` is not the desired conclusion and already holds in the strongest
  issue #3 countermodel;
- unweighted derivative stability is not justified;
- no canonical different/discriminant multiplier has been shown to turn
  (4.3) into a unit-value theorem.
