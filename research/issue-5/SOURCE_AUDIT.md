## 1. Differents and the monogenic formula

### The Stacks Project, Tag 0BVZ

- **Statement used:** Lemma 49.7.4, for
  \(B=A[x_1,\ldots,x_n]/(f_1,\ldots,f_n)\), identifies the Kähler different with
  the ideal generated by the Jacobian determinant
  \(\det(\partial f_i/\partial x_j)\).
- **Specialization:** for a monogenic algebra \(A[T]/(f)\), the Kähler different
  is generated by \(f'(s)\).
- **Stable identifier:** <https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BVZ>
- **Application:** Section 3.1 of the principal-parts packet.
- **Boundary:** this does not say that an arbitrary primitive field element
  generates the integral closure.

### The Stacks Project, Tags 0BWE and 0BWG

- **Statements used:** Tag 0BWE gives the equivalent quasi-finite syntomic /
  quasi-finite flat lci hypotheses. Tag 0BWG, Lemma 49.12.3, identifies the
  scheme-theoretic different with the Kähler different under those hypotheses.
- **Stable identifiers:**  
  <https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BWE>  
  <https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BWG>
- **Application:** the phrase “\(f'(s)\) generates the different” is used only
  in a monogenic finite flat lci neighborhood.
- **Boundary:** where that presentation is unavailable, the packet uses the
  invariant condition \(\widetilde V(S)\subseteq S\).

## 2. Tame ramification and the exponent \(e-1\)

### The Stacks Project, Tag 09E9

- **Statement used:** Definition 15.113.7 declares a finite separable DVR
  extension tame when the residue characteristic is zero, or in positive
  residue characteristic when the residue extension is separable and the
  ramification indices are prime to the characteristic.
- **Stable identifier:** <https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/09E9>
- **Application:** all divisorial extensions over \(\mathbf C\) are tame.

### The Stacks Project, Tag 0C1F

- **Statement used:** Lemma 53.12.4 identifies the different/ramification
  multiplicity with the length of relative differentials, proves
  \(d\ge e-1\), and gives equality exactly in the tame case.
- **Stable identifier:** <https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0C1F>
- **Application:** at every height-one ramified point in the present
  characteristic-zero setting,
  \[
  d=e-1.
  \]
- **Boundary:** the cited lemma is stated in the finite-curve/DVR context; the
  packet applies it to the corresponding transversal DVR extension at a
  codimension-one point.

The local formula \(u=\pi^e\) is used after completion and an unramified base
change as a standard tame normal form. The invariant conclusion
\(V(u)\in(u)\) is independent of that auxiliary base change.

## 3. Normality, height-one intersections, and reflexivity

### The Stacks Project, Tag 031T

- **Statement used:** Lemma 10.157.6(2) states that a noetherian normal domain
  \(A\) with fraction field \(K\) satisfies
  \[
  A=\bigcap_{\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak p=1}A_{\mathfrak p}
  \quad\text{inside }K.
  \]
- **Stable identifier:** <https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/031T>
- **Application:** if a rational derivation is regular at every height-one
  localization, it sends every element of \(A\) into \(A\). This is the
  codimension-two extension step in Section 7.1.
- **Hypotheses checked:** affine-local rings of \(Y\) are noetherian normal
  domains by construction.

### The Stacks Project, Tags 0AVB and 0AVC

- **Statements used:** Lemmas 15.24.18 and 15.24.19 characterize finite
  reflexive modules over a normal noetherian domain by their height-one
  localizations and identify the reflexive hull as the corresponding
  intersection.
- **Stable identifiers:**  
  <https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0AVB>  
  <https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0AVC>
- **Application:** conceptual identification of the codimension-one-controlled
  tangent sheaf. The actual derivation extension is also proved directly from
  Tag 031T, so no unsupported smoothness assertion is needed.

## 4. Exact equivariant terminal theorem

### T. Shaska, *Graded Keller maps and the Jacobian Conjecture*

- **Version audited:** arXiv:2607.20210v1, submitted 22 July 2026.
- **Stable identifier:** <https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20210>
- **Exact result used conditionally:** Theorem 3.3 states that a Keller map
  \(G:\mathbf C^2\to\mathbf C^2\) equivariant for nontrivial algebraic
  \(\mathbf G_m\)-actions on source and target is an automorphism. It further
  classifies the diagonalized forms by the sign pattern of the weights.
- **Hypotheses that must still be proved before use here:**
  1. the regular derivation on \(Y\) integrates to an algebraic
     \(\mathbf G_m\)-action;
  2. the source open \(U\) is invariant;
  3. the induced action on \(U\simeq\mathbf A^2\) is nontrivial;
  4. \(F\) is equivariant for the source and target actions.
- **What is not supplied:** regularity, completeness, or local finiteness of a
  rationally lifted derivation.

### A. Gutwirth, *The action of an algebraic torus on the affine plane*

- **Citation:** A. Gutwirth, *Transactions of the American Mathematical
  Society* **105** (1962), 407–414, DOI 10.2307/1993728.
- **Use:** Shaska's proof of Theorem 3.3 invokes the linearizability of an
  algebraic torus action on \(\mathbf A^2\).
- **Scope boundary:** this is an action-linearization theorem. It does not turn
  an arbitrary regular derivation into an algebraic action and is not invoked
  before integration has been established.
