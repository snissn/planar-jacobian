# Track J — Equivariant Degeneration

> Status: `MUTABLE_NONAUTHORITATIVE`

The three-dimensional example shows how a residual torus can organize a nonproper étale cover. In the plane, exact nontrivial `G_m`-equivariance is now source-bound to Shaska's arXiv:2607.20210 and forces a Keller map to be an automorphism for every sign pattern of the weights.

The unresolved problem is therefore not exact symmetry but **no-escape passage from a general map to an exact graded representative**.

## Two degeneration scales

### Coefficient-space closed orbit

A torus limit of a bounded-degree Keller map can become an automorphism, lower function-field degree, or discard a boundary valuation. A useful degeneration must remain inside a stratum with fixed:

- function-field degree;
- boundary dual graph;
- valuation key sequences;
- ramification and inertia data.

### Weighted Rees staircase

Track [`m-filtered-equivariance.md`](m-filtered-equivariance.md) fixes a positive source weight and retains all weighted layers in a Rees family. The constant Jacobian travels through a finite staircase rather than surviving only in the top associated-graded map.

The conversation-derived reduction through grading defect `3` is a candidate. Defect `4` is the first blocked case because of a middle Wronskian term; see [`L13-defect-4-staircase.md`](../leaf-packets/L13-defect-4-staircase.md).

## Missing no-escape theorem

A full route needs a well-founded invariant such that a filtration-compatible polynomial transformation:

1. preserves `J(P,Q)=1`;
2. lowers the invariant;
3. does not delete a boundary valuation or lower the generic degree;
4. terminates at an exactly equivariant map covered by the graded theorem.

A closed coefficient-space orbit would give a global version. A staircase reduction would give a constructive filtered version. Neither is established.

## Exit

This track exits through either:

- a proof that a minimal counterexample has a closed torus orbit inside a fixed boundary stratum; or
- a terminating Rees-staircase reduction preserving the nonzero bracket and boundary data.

A defect-4 result is a scoped contribution to the second route, not a proof of the full exit statement.
