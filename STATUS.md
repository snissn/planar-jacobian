## Synchronized conclusions

### Literature-backed

1. A planar Keller map that is exactly equivariant for a nontrivial algebraic `G_m` grading is an automorphism for every sign pattern of the weights. The current source is T. Shaska, *Graded Keller maps and the Jacobian Conjecture*, arXiv:2607.20210.
2. The planar problem can be reformulated through the canonical pulled-back affine Lie algebra generated by the Hamiltonian translation fields; algebraicity/completeness of that action would force invertibility.
3. The finite normalization, boundary-at-infinity, Newton-polygon, Brieskorn/Gauss--Manin, and monodromy approaches all identify nonproper sheet loss at infinity as the surviving obstruction.

### Candidate analytic results requiring documented adversarial audit

1. **Weighted Rees staircase identity.** For a positive primitive weight `w=(p,q)`, if
   
   `kappa_w = deg_w(P) + deg_w(Q) - p - q`,
   
   the weighted Rees deformation satisfies
   
   `J(Pcal,Qcal)=t^{kappa_w}`,
   
   yielding layer equations
   
   `sum_{i+j=n} J(P_i,Q_j)=0` for `n<kappa_w`, and `=1` for `n=kappa_w`.
2. **Resonant layer.** At least one pair of weighted layers with indices summing to `kappa_w` has nonzero constant Jacobian and is therefore an exactly graded Keller automorphism.
3. **Low-defect candidate.** The conversation-derived argument claims that `kappa_w <= 3` forces the original map to be an automorphism. This has not received a documented adversarial review at a pinned repository revision and is not promoted as a theorem.

### First unresolved filtered case

At `kappa_w=4`, after normalizing an interior resonant pair, the first new equation has the form

```text
J(P_0,Q_2) + J(P_1,Q_1) + J(P_2,Q_0) = 0.
```

The middle Wronskian `J(P_1,Q_1)` is absent in the defect-2 argument and is the first term capable of carrying nontrivial curvature, cusp data, or boundary monodromy.
