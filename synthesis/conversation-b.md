# Conversation B Synthesis

Conversation B is a shorter parallel exploration of the same problem. It emphasizes the passage from visible symmetry in the three-dimensional example to unavoidable **infinitesimal** symmetry in every planar Keller map.

The central objects are the canonical derivations

`D_P = Q_y ∂_x - Q_x ∂_y` and `D_Q = -P_y ∂_x + P_x ∂_y`,

which commute and lift target translations. Their Euler and hyperbolic combinations would integrate to algebraic actions if no boundary poles intervened. This turns the conjecture into a completeness or local-finiteness problem for a canonical frame.

Conversation B also develops the finite-normalization factorization, free boundary-class group heuristic, generic canonical-divisor coefficients, exact symplectic identity, generic-fiber punctures, and the idea that a counterexample must concentrate its failure in codimension-two gluing at infinity.

Its durable contribution is conceptual unification: visible deck symmetry is not necessary, but canonical infinitesimal symmetry is automatic. The obstruction is precisely the failure of that symmetry to globalize across the normalization boundary.

As with Conversation A, external attributions and several strong boundary assertions require source-level audit before use.
