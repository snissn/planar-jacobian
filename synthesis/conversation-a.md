# Conversation A Synthesis

## Scope

Conversation A begins with the announced three-dimensional polynomial counterexample and progressively uncovers its structure: weighted coordinates, a marked-root cubic, resultant-one factorization, the incidence map `P1 × Sym2(P1) -> Sym3(P1)`, the residual torus grading, and a geometric route to the affine-space chart. It then turns to the still-open planar case and develops many parallel closure attempts.

## Three-dimensional construction imported as context

The durable conceptual picture is that a target binary cubic is paired with a chosen simple root. The root determines a normalized linear factor; the complementary quadratic factor is scaled so that the resultant is one. Forgetting the root is generically three-to-one. When a root becomes repeated, the corresponding normalized factorization runs to infinity rather than producing a finite critical point.

This explains the constant Jacobian, the generic degree three, the discriminant/nonproperness hypersurface, the visible torus weights, and the explicit collisions. It also illustrates the key planar obstruction: local étaleness does not prevent sheets from escaping through a deleted boundary.

## Planar proof program developed in the conversation

The planar discussion repeatedly reformulates the same obstruction:

- finite normalization and divisors at infinity;
- canonical commuting derivations and incomplete flows;
- global primitive elements and moving index divisors;
- stable differential orders and discriminant ideals;
- generic-fiber punctures and Gauss–Manin modules;
- quasi-Albanese nonproperness;
- one-boundary Wright rings and graded reduction;
- Galois closure, meridian monodromy, and cusp braids;
- exact symplectic boundary constraints;
- equivariant degeneration and characteristic-p ideas.

The conversation produced useful candidate lemmas, but it also changed or withdrew several claims. The claim ledger separates these states.

## Most important surviving reductions

The strongest recurring sufficient conditions are:

1. a global primitive element for the finite normalization;
2. a finite order stable under both canonical lifted translations;
3. regular extension of one canonical radial or affine flow;
4. finiteness of the intrinsic quasi-Albanese map;
5. vanishing of the generic-fiber puncture module;
6. a simultaneous graded reduction in the one-boundary model.

Any one of these would reduce the function-field degree to one under the relevant auxiliary theorems.

## Honest boundary

No unconditional proof is contained in Conversation A. The remaining gaps are global: local data can be normalized or generated, but the local choices acquire moving divisors, incompatible valuations, punctures, or monodromy when one tries to globalize them.
